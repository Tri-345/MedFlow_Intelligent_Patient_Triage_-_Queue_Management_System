# MedFlow_Intelligent_Patient_Triage_-_Queue_Management_System

## Overview
An AI-supported digital patient triage and queue management prototype, developed as a team capstone project aligned with **UN SDG 3: Good Health and Well-Being**. Case study: University of Benin Teaching Hospital (UBTH), Benin City, Edo State, Nigeria.

## Problem Statement
Many Nigerian public hospitals, including UBTH, serve patients on a first-come, first-served basis regardless of medical urgency. Manual triage and limited visibility into patient flow contribute to overcrowding, long wait times, and poorer outcomes, especially for critically ill patients waiting behind non-urgent cases.

## Proposed Solution
MedFlow enables digital pre-triage before consultation. Patients complete a structured symptom assessment, processed by an AI-assisted classification layer working alongside rule-based clinical logic to categorize each patient as **Emergency**, **Urgent**, or **Routine**. Hospital staff access a live dashboard showing prioritized queues, patient flow analytics, and AI-generated insights such as predicted wait times and overcrowding risk.

## Results and Findings
The project confirmed that UBTH's manual, first-come-first-served triage process was a key driver of overcrowding and delayed care, with critically ill patients frequently waiting behind less urgent cases. In prototype testing, the AI-assisted classification layer was able to sort patients into Emergency, Urgent, and Routine categories faster than manual assessment, reducing the risk of urgent patients being deprioritized. The staff-facing Power BI dashboard gave hospital staff real-time visibility into queue length, patient flow, and predicted wait times, insights that were not previously available through the hospital's existing manual process. Within the project timeline of 3–7 July 2026, the team successfully delivered a working prototype, full documentation, and a Demo Day presentation, demonstrating how data analytics and AI can directly support healthcare operations and improve patient outcomes in a real, resource-constrained hospital setting.
